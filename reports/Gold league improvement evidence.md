# Earlier catches, not smoke tweaks, win Gold

Of the nine ideas, the Gold evidence supports four only in a narrower form than proposed and rejects the rest, and the ones that matter for rank are those that produce catches sooner, not those that change where or when smoke is thrown. **Thirty per cent of Gold games end level on catches and 91 % of those are settled by the turn of the first catch; v10 makes its first catch on turn 11 (median) against turn 8 for the field and turn 6 for winners.** The best-supported changes are, in order: an opening in which unthreatened hunters spread over different prey and keep going at prey that is not on the predator's side; a smoke cloud that is already up when a predator's net lands, with the trapped unit netting its hunter at once and never sending a decoy; and ghosts used as eyes on prey that no living hunter is near. Smoke between us and the predator, leaving our own cloud, a later smoke trigger, netting the hunter before fleeing, forgetting old prey positions and hiding behind a tree have no support, and the later smoke trigger was worse on 36 replayed Gold battles. The evidence is mostly observational: 2,200 to 2,500 Gold games of all teams, our 80 arena games and 65 debug replays of them. Only four candidates have been replayed against real opponents so far; three were worse than v10 and one was level. By rough arithmetic no single item is worth more than a few hundredths of a rank place, so the gap to the top closes by adding several of them, each proven on replayed battles before it goes live.

## Replays overrule the local referee, and restrictions cost catches

Four kinds of evidence are used below, and they do not carry the same weight. **Gold arena logs of all teams** (2,176 to 2,533 games depending on the note, because the download was still running) show what goes with being caught or catching across thousands of cases, but they are comparisons inside observed play: a bot smokes or nets when it judges a unit to be in danger, so the group that acts differs from the group that does not. Tree positions, sight and power pools are not in those logs. **Our own Gold games** (79 to 80 arena games, plus 65 debug replays covering 47 distinct battles) give exact positions, sight and pools, but every split of 65 games has a standard error near 0.2 of a rank place. **Replay tests** put changed code back into battles v10 really played, against the same opponents, seats and map; they are exactly reproducible and are the only test here against real Gold bots. **The local referee** plays our own versions against each other.

The local referee cannot accept an idea. Its verdict depends on which versions sit in the pool by more than the effects being tested. The clearest case is the smoke trigger: v10 smokes when a predator is within 2,300, and the variant that waits until 1,800 was the best in one pool, level in another and the worst in two pools that also contained older versions of the bot ([pools1.log](E:/cg-tmp/tcf/sub/pools1.log), [pools2.log](E:/cg-tmp/tcf/sub/pools2.log), [pools3.log](E:/cg-tmp/tcf/sub/pools3.log), [pools4.log](E:/cg-tmp/tcf/sub/pools4.log)). One standard error is about 0.05, so the same change swung from 0.17 better to 0.37 worse on pool membership alone. Local results are therefore used below only to reject clear losers and to check for crashes (there were none in any pool).

| Test of "smoke only when the predator is within 1,800" (mean rank, lower is better) | v10 unchanged | 1,800 trigger |
|---|---|---|
| Local pool of smoke variants, 288 games each | 2.05 | 1.88 |
| Local pool of five trigger distances, 576 games each | 1.98 | 1.99 |
| Local pool with one older version | 1.84 | 2.21 |
| Local pool with two older versions | 1.73 | 2.07 |
| **Replay of 36 real Gold battles** | **1.83** | **2.06** |

The replay tests so far share one pattern. The 1,800 trigger left units lost unchanged (2.53 against 2.47 per game) and cut catches from 3.22 to 2.72; a rule that sent the second hunter to a point behind the prey was the largest local gain of the day and lost on 24 real battles (mean rank 2.04 against 1.88, catches 2.75 against 3.25) ([log.md, replay table](<E:/New folder/prep/try-catch-forest/kb/log.md>)). A cap of 1,600 on net range was stopped after 10 battles at 2.00 against 1.50, better in none and worse in three, with catches at 2.80 against 3.60 (running count from the test session; the log file still shows the first three battles). **All three lost catches and rank followed; in every case tested, a rule that restricted an action cost catches.** A fourth candidate, built on rescue nets by team mates, was level over 26 battles. Two cautions apply. The samples are small: the 36-battle result is better in 10 battles, worse in 12 and the same in 14, and the test session puts the standard error of such a comparison at 0.15 to 0.2 on 25 battles, so a difference of 0.23 is about one and a half standard errors ([gold.md, sections 9 and 10](<E:/New folder/prep/try-catch-forest/kb/gold.md>)). And one more candidate, which lets a hunter always dash onto prey that is held by a net, was still being replayed when this report was written; it has no result.

Two things qualify the order. The notes decline to convert these sizes into rank, because the coefficients are associations and overlap; rough arithmetic from their own figures puts each item at a few hundredths of a place. And the reason tempo sits above the defensive items is an inference, not a measurement: in the smoke-trigger replay units lost stayed flat while half a catch fewer per game went with a mean rank worse by 0.23, and in our own 80 Gold games 32 of 44 non-wins went to the team we hunt, not to the team that hunts us ([gold-autopsy.md, section 1](<E:/New folder/prep/try-catch-forest/kb/gold-autopsy.md>)).

## Unthreatened hunters, not braver ones, bring the first catch forward

The value of the first catch is as large as the owner supposed. **The top two teams are level on catches in 339 of 1,126 games (30.1 %), and 310 of those ties are decided by the first catch, with a median gap of 9 turns** ([a_top.out](E:/cg-tmp/tcf/research_tempo/a_top.out)). The team that makes the first catch of the game wins 54 % of the time, 60 % with three units per team. But an early loss costs exactly as much as an early catch gains: one more catch by turn 10 is worth 0.28 of a place and one more unit lost by turn 10 costs 0.28 ([a_top.out](E:/cg-tmp/tcf/research_tempo/a_top.out)). A change that buys a catch for a unit is a wash.

That is why aggression in its blunt form fails. A variant that until turn 10 kept less distance from predators, smoked only inside 1,500 and dashed in more boldly scored 2.07 against 1.96 on the local referee and caught less (1.95 against 2.26 per game), a clear loser ([pools1.log](E:/cg-tmp/tcf/sub/pools1.log)). The all-team logs say the same: for a unit that starts within 2,000 of a predator, smoke on turn 1 goes with 21.7 % lost by turn 10 against 39.5 % and a rank better by 0.18, over 496 matched triples of mirrored units ([b2_all.out](E:/cg-tmp/tcf/research_tempo/b2_all.out)). v10 already smokes there in 74 % of cases, as the leader does. The early smoke must stay.

The extra early catches of the best teams come from the units that are not threatened, and v10 differs from them in four measurable ways ([i_us.out](E:/cg-tmp/tcf/research_tempo/i_us.out), [b_top.out](E:/cg-tmp/tcf/research_tempo/b_top.out)). First, **v10 has a hunter within 1,500 of only 0.59 different prey units at the start of turn 4, against 0.84 for the field and 0.98 for the teams that lead on catches by turn 10**; our opponents on the same maps reach 0.68, so part of the gap is the mix of maps. Each further prey unit covered goes with 0.13 more catches and 0.11 fewer losses by turn 10, and three or more units sent at one prey on turn 1 go with a rank worse by 0.16 to 0.23. Second, v10 throws 0.59 nets at prey on turns 2 and 3 against 0.90 for the tempo leaders; a net on turn 2 hits 55 % and becomes a catch 33 % of the time, against 28 % and 15 % on turn 1, when v10 throws none. Third, v10 dashes 0.40 times per unit in turns 2 to 5 against 0.61 for the field. Fourth, a unit of ours that starts next to a predator dashes on turn 1 in 17 % of cases against 78 % for the field and 100 % for the leader; dashing goes with 27.2 % lost by turn 10 against 38.9 %, and dashing toward the side where prey starts loses the unit less often than dashing straight away (26.3 % against 34.8 %) ([b2_all.out](E:/cg-tmp/tcf/research_tempo/b2_all.out)).

The fourth point has one piece of contrary evidence. A local variant that always dashed away and smoked on turn 1 scored 1.99 against 1.84 ([pools3.log](E:/cg-tmp/tcf/sub/pools3.log)). In that pool an older version of the bot came first at 1.75, ahead of v10, which is the pool effect described above; and the variant dashed straight away, the direction the logs rate lower. With smoke already thrown, the logs themselves have only 22 matched cases of dash against no dash. The idea is open rather than supported or rejected.

The same logic holds after the opening. When a unit has both a prey and a predator within 2,500, v10 goes at the prey in 46 % of unit-turns against 57 to 61 % for the field, and the gap is widest when the prey lies at a right angle to the predator (41 % against 58 to 64 %). In that geometry going on is not more dangerous than turning away (9.5 % caught within five turns against 12.3 %) and the prey is caught 26.7 % of the time against 10.6 %. **The one dangerous case is prey on the predator's side with the predator inside 1,200: 37 % caught against 22 %**, confirmed in both data sets ([c_top.out](E:/cg-tmp/tcf/research_tempo/c_top.out), [c_old.out](E:/cg-tmp/tcf/research_tempo/c_old.out)). This is the aggression the data supports: not a shorter distance to predators, but no longer giving up prey that is not on the predator's side. Units that flee may be the ones whose predator is really coming, so the equal risk is an upper bound on how safe continuing is.

## A trapped unit should net at once; the decoy step fails at every distance

A unit is trapped by a predator's net 1.25 times per team-game across 2,176 games, and 66 % of those units are caught within five turns; for v10 it happens 1.38 times per game ([held.out](E:/cg-tmp/tcf/research_nets/held.out), [ours.out](E:/cg-tmp/tcf/research_nets/ours.out)). Thirty per cent are caught on the very next turn, before any response can act. For the rest, the proposed order of net, then decoy, then smoke is supported for its first step only.

| First response of a trapped unit that survives the next turn | Cases | Caught in the following four turns |
|---|---|---|
| Net at the hunter | 1,016 | **37 %** |
| Nothing | 3,102 | 52 % |
| Smoke | 1,068 | 56 % |
| Decoy | 385 | **68 %** |

The order of these four holds inside every class of hunter distance ([held.out, tables 3b and 4b](E:/cg-tmp/tcf/research_nets/held.out)). The net should go at once, also on an unsure aim: such nets hit 78 % of the time because the hunter walks straight at a unit that cannot move, a miss leaves the unit no worse off than doing nothing (53 %), and waiting one turn for a better aim ends in 67 % caught. v10 already does this when it sees the hunter: 11 nets thrown, 11 hits, 2 units lost. A decoy is worse than doing nothing, because the other teams see two units with one id, only one of them trapped, and a decoy does not block a catch. The local referee agrees: a trapped unit that sends a decoy before it smokes scored 2.04 against 1.93 ([pools1.log](E:/cg-tmp/tcf/sub/pools1.log)). The exact three-step sequence occurs only 19 times in the logs (42 % caught, weak), against 13 % for "net, then nothing" over 464 cases.

Smoke works before the net, not after it. **A unit that is already inside a cloud when the net lands is caught 47 % of the time against 82 % in the open when a hunter can arrive next turn** (949 and 4,411 cases); a smoke ordered one turn after the hit changes nothing while the hunter is within about 1,640 ([held2.out, tables 15 and 15b](E:/cg-tmp/tcf/research_nets/held2.out)). A second, independent count gives 43 % against 68 % overall, and 74 % for smoke ordered after the hit ([out_net.txt](E:/cg-tmp/tcf/research_smoke/out_net.txt)). Late smoke helps only when no hunter can arrive within two turns: 30 % caught against 47 %, and 21 % against 30 % when none is within 3,000. This is where v10 has gaps. In our 39 debug replays the smoke was already used up in 21 of 54 cases and 19 of those units were caught; with smoke left, 14 of 33. And v10 did nothing in 16 of the 19 cases where no hunter was within two turns ([ours.out](E:/cg-tmp/tcf/research_nets/ours.out)). Units in a cloud are also harder to net at all, so the 35-point difference overstates what a rule can win; the note's own estimate is about 0.13 units per game.

Netting the approaching hunter before fleeing (B5) is a different situation and the data rejects it. A free unit with a predator inside 1,500 is caught within five turns 26 % of the time when it nets that predator and 24 % when it uses no power (2,249 and 39,150 cases); a third of those nets miss, the thrower is itself netted 32 % of the time against 7 %, and a dash away alone reaches 18 % ([flee.out](E:/cg-tmp/tcf/research_nets/flee.out)). Locally it was level (1.92 against 1.93). A net helps a unit that cannot move and does not help one that can. Rescue nets by team mates work when they hit (47 % caught against 62 %) but a mate is within 2,500 in only 0.39 cases per team-game, and the earlier candidate that included them was level over 26 replayed battles.

On prediction (idea 2), v10 has nothing to gain from a new aim rule. The best simple rule in the logs is full range through the target's position plus half its last movement (76 % hits on targets whose dash is on cooldown, over 105,146 chances), and v10's throws score 64 % against 65 % for that rule in the same model ([aim2.out](E:/cg-tmp/tcf/research_nets/aim2.out), [ours.out](E:/cg-tmp/tcf/research_nets/ours.out)). Predicting a flight straight away from the hunter does worse (72 %), and aiming a whole last movement ahead after a dash hits 30 %. What separates the best throwers is the target: 65 % hits on a target whose dash is on cooldown, 73 % on a held one, 35 % on one that can dash, and 24 % on turn 1 ([aim.out](E:/cg-tmp/tcf/research_nets/aim.out)). Half of v10's nets go at dash-ready targets. In our own small sample that costs almost nothing (47 % against 49 % on prey), and the two replay tests that restricted nets both lost catches, so this belongs in the bot as a preference between two available targets, never as a ban on throwing.

## Smoke stops nets, not predators that are already coming

The smoke questions resolve once the cloud's real effect is clear. From turn 4 on, a free unit that orders smoke is never caught less often than a unit at the same distance that does not, in any distance bucket, also when only units with a predator moving straight at them are compared ([out_timing.txt](E:/cg-tmp/tcf/research_smoke/out_timing.txt), [out_more.txt](E:/cg-tmp/tcf/research_smoke/out_more.txt)). What falls is the rate of being netted: 0.7 to 1.2 % per three turns inside an own cloud against 2.9 to 4.9 % outside. The one place where smokers clearly survive better is turns 1 to 3 with a predator coming: 22.8 % caught against 35.7 % (1,102 and 6,741 cases). Bots smoke the units they judge endangered, so "no effect against walkers" is probably overstated, but the direction is consistent across both data sets.

**Smoke thrown with the predator very near (idea 4) is wasted in two cases and not in a third.** With the predator inside 700, 14.8 % of the orders die with the thrower and the rest are caught as often as non-smokers (31.8 % against 31.2 %); v10 spends 0.27 smokes per game there, and a local variant that withheld smoke inside 800 was level (1.955 against 1.958) ([out_timing.txt](E:/cg-tmp/tcf/research_smoke/out_timing.txt), [pools1.log](E:/cg-tmp/tcf/sub/pools1.log)). Smoke by a trapped unit after the net has hit is wasted while the hunter is close, as shown above. But smoke combined with a dash is the safest response measured inside about 1,040: 17 % caught against 22 % for a dash alone and 34 to 37 % for walking with or without smoke ([flee.out, tables C2 and C3](E:/cg-tmp/tcf/research_nets/flee.out)). Near smoke is wasted when the unit walks or cannot move, not when it dashes.

**Throwing the cloud between us and the predator (idea 5, B3) has no support.** Placement is a habit of each team, so it can only be compared between teams, and no placement protects the thrower clearly better. Relative to what the distances of the throws predict, teams that throw away from the predator lose 0.91 of the expected throwers, "between" teams 0.90, teams that smoke themselves 0.98, and v10, which throws 900 ahead of its own move, 0.76 ([out_team.txt](E:/cg-tmp/tcf/research_smoke/out_team.txt)). The leader does throw between and walk out (ratio 0.58), but on 57 games, and it is moving away from the cloud on 92 % of those throws, which may be the real cause. Covering the predator is the one placement that is measurably worse: more throwers netted on the throw turn (7.3 % against 4.0 to 5.8 %) and the worst team ranks ([out_place.txt](E:/cg-tmp/tcf/research_smoke/out_place.txt), [out_netjoin.txt](E:/cg-tmp/tcf/research_smoke/out_netjoin.txt)); our own earlier rule that threw the cloud past the hunter was harmful on the local referee, since the hunter becomes invisible to us too ([gold.md, section 3](<E:/New folder/prep/try-catch-forest/kb/gold.md>)). Leaving the cloud sooner gains little: throwers that leave are caught slightly less (11.6 % against 13.7 %) but catch less and are netted three to five times as often outside. Locally the "between" variant was level on rank and lost more units (2.46 against 2.26 per game), and centring on the thrower was level ([pools1.log](E:/cg-tmp/tcf/sub/pools1.log)).

The single-battle hypotheses about smoke follow from this. Leaving our cloud when unthreatened (B1) rests on a premise the data contradicts: units inside an own cloud catch more at the same prey distance (21.8 % against 12.6 % within three turns at 1,000 to 1,500, all teams), and our rank is flat between games with little and much time inside (1.66 against 1.70) ([out_more.txt](E:/cg-tmp/tcf/research_smoke/out_more.txt), [out_own.txt](E:/cg-tmp/tcf/research_smoke/out_own.txt)). The battle that raised it had three times our usual share of turns inside. A later trigger (B2) is supported only at the far end: beyond a true distance of 2,300 a non-smoker is caught within five turns in under 5 % of cases, and we throw 0.23 smokes per game there, some probably at decoys. Between 1,500 and 2,300 the logs are undecided and the replay test is not. It helps to know that v10's 2,300 is measured to our position after our move; by the predator's true distance at the start of the turn our median throw is at 1,379, the same as the leader's 1,396, so v10 is not early. No smoke on turns 1 to 3 with prey nearer (B4) is right but concerns 0.1 to 0.2 throws per game, and one cloud per group (B7) saves about 0.15: a second cloud within 1,900 of a fresh one leaves the thrower caught exactly as often (20 % against 20 %) ([out_smoke2.txt](E:/cg-tmp/tcf/research_smoke/out_smoke2.txt)). A local bundle of the 1,800 trigger with four of the single-battle rules (leave the cloud, no early smoke, net the hunter first, forget old prey positions) was the best of its pool (1.87 against 1.97), but it is built on the trigger the replay rejected and each part alone was level within the noise, so it proves nothing ([pools2.log](E:/cg-tmp/tcf/sub/pools2.log)).

Following a plan inside smoke (idea 1) addresses a real defect with an unproven cost. Inside our cloud, threatened units loop: in 21 % of three-turn windows they end less than half as far away as they walked, against 2 % outside, and the loopers are then caught 15 % of the time against 10 %, a difference inside the noise (16 of 110 against 14 of 143) ([out_own.txt](E:/cg-tmp/tcf/research_smoke/out_own.txt)). The earlier autopsy found that 79 of our 186 lost units had no known predator within 2,500 and three quarters of those stood in a cloud, while the bot forgets a predator after 3 turns and a cloud lasts 9 ([gold-autopsy.md](<E:/New folder/prep/try-catch-forest/kb/gold-autopsy.md>)). Yet the one fix tried, remembering predators for 9 turns while a unit is in a cloud, was a local loser at 2.10 against 1.95 ([pools3.log](E:/cg-tmp/tcf/sub/pools3.log)). The attack half of the idea has no case: units in a cloud already catch more than units outside it. What remains worth a test is narrow: a threatened unit in our cloud keeps one heading away from the last known predator position instead of turning.

## Ghosts find far prey; the endgame decides 2 % of games

**Hiding behind a tree once nothing is left to catch (idea 7) is not worth a test slot.** A team that has caught all its prey plays on in 53 % of games, but its tie-break against the remaining hunter is already won in 72 % of those, and first place is lost in 51 of 2,533 games, 2.0 % ([d_all.out](E:/cg-tmp/tcf/research_tempo/d_all.out)). Where tree positions are known (our debug replays), a tree on the line to a predator helps only at 1,200 to 2,000 (6.0 % caught against 12.2 %, 134 cases), and standing within 300 of a tree with a predator inside 1,200 is the worst place measured (24.5 % against 9.4 % in open ground), because the tree blocks the escape ([h_own.out](E:/cg-tmp/tcf/research_tempo/h_own.out)). The local "hide" variant produced the same rank to three decimals ([pools1.log](E:/cg-tmp/tcf/sub/pools1.log)). If a rule is wanted it is cheap: stand still only while no predator is within about 3,000, then move straight away, stay off the wall, and throw the left-over nets at the hunter. The mirror seat is worth far more. When the team that hunts us is eliminated nothing can catch us, and teams in that seat add 0.93 catches and gain a place in 21 % of those endgames; that seat occurs in 17.6 % of team-games, and how v10 plays it has not been measured.

For ghosts (idea 8) the evidence is consistent and modest. A ghost is the best scout in the game: **a prey unit with one of our ghosts within 2,000 is in our sight 91 % of the time, against 60 to 67 % with a living unit at that distance** ([h_own.out](E:/cg-tmp/tcf/research_tempo/h_own.out)). Across all teams a ghost next to prey whose nearest hunter is more than 5,000 away raises the chance that it is caught within 20 turns from 8.4 % to 11.0 % (6.9 % to 10.5 % in the older set); next to prey that hunters already follow it changes nothing ([f_top.out](E:/cg-tmp/tcf/research_tempo/f_top.out), [f_old.out](E:/cg-tmp/tcf/research_tempo/f_old.out)). The share of ghost time spent near living predators is the one that goes with winning (26 % wins in the lowest third, 37 % in the highest), plausibly because a ghost on a predator shows it to our living units. v10 already keeps 49 % of its ghost-turns near prey, so the gain is in the assignment: far prey and the last one or two prey first, one ghost on the nearest predator, none idle. A local variant that sent idle ghosts to search the map was level (1.93 against 1.88). Two limits apply: with one prey left we see it on 53 % of turns and still catch only once per 106 turns, so finishing limits the last catch more than finding does; and ghosts appear where units die, which is where the action already is, so the association is not an experiment.

Forgetting remembered prey positions sooner (B6) changes almost nothing. Only 34 of 1,365 hunting orders given without prey in sight came more than 8 turns after the last sighting, blind hunting is more common in our wins than in our non-wins (47 % against 41 % of orders), and the local variant was level (1.94 against 1.93) ([h_own.out](E:/cg-tmp/tcf/research_tempo/h_own.out), [pools1.log](E:/cg-tmp/tcf/sub/pools1.log)).

## Conclusion

Three things changed in this pass. The battle that raised B1 to B7 was an outlier: four of its seven hypotheses are rejected, one survives only at its far end, and the two that hold are worth a tenth of a smoke per game each; smoke is where v10 is already close to the best teams, not where it trails them. The trapped-unit proposal was right about urgency and wrong about the tools: the net is the only response that works after the hit, and smoke has to be there before it. And the rules that lost on real battles restricted or redirected actions and paid in catches, while the best-supported items here add actions (more prey covered, more dashes, a net on turn 2, ghosts that work) or remove only actions that show no effect at all. That asymmetry is the most useful guide for choosing what to build. Each item below still needs its own switch, a local run to catch crashes and clear losers, and then 30 or more replayed Gold battles counted as pairs; items 1 to 3 produce the same early catches and must be tested one at a time.

| Order | Build and test next | Why first |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Unthreatened hunters spread over different prey by turn 4 (never three on one) and keep going at prey that is beside or opposite the predator; turn away only when the prey is on the predator's side and the predator is inside 1,200 | Largest gap to the field, every game, adds catches |
| 2 | First net on turn 2 or 3 at prey inside about 1,500 or prey that has just dashed, and a second dash by turn 3; written as a preference, not a ban | Turn-2 nets hit twice as often as turn-1 nets |
| 3 | A unit that starts next to a predator dashes on turn 1 as well as smoking, toward the side where prey starts | v10 17 %, field 78 %; the local test of "straight away" was negative, so try the other direction |
| 4 | Trapped unit: net again after a miss, smoke on itself when no hunter can arrive within two turns, no smoke after the hit otherwise; stop smokes beyond 2,300 true distance and second clouds beside a fresh one, so that smoke is left for the moment a net threatens | Returns about 1 smoke per game to the one use that shows an effect |
| 5 | Ghosts: one on each prey unit with no living hunter within 3,000, the last prey first; one on the predator nearest our living units | Cheap, no unit at risk |
| 6 | When the team that hunts us is eliminated: no fleeing, no saving of dashes, every net thrown | 17.6 % of games, never measured for v10 |
| - | Read the result of the replay now running (always dash onto prey held by a net) before adding net rules | An additive finishing rule; the only candidate in test |

Drop: smoke between us and the predator or centred on ourselves; leaving our own cloud when unthreatened; a smoke trigger of 1,800 or 1,500; netting the approaching hunter before fleeing; forgetting prey positions after 8 turns; a decoy from a trapped unit; hiding behind a tree as a rank item; any new aim predictor; the second hunter aiming behind the prey; a hard cap on net range; nets on turn 1; rescue nets by team mates; and "more aggression" in the sense of standing closer to predators or smoking later.
